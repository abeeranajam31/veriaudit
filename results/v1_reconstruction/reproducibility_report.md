# VeriAudit v0.1 — Reproducibility Report

Generated by `research/v1_reconstruction/reconstruct.py` (run:
`PYTHONPATH=backend python research/v1_reconstruction/reconstruct.py`).
Statistical primitives: `backend/app/v2/stats.py` (unit-tested in
`backend/tests/test_v2_stats_and_judge.py`). No v1 file was modified; all
outputs are new files in this directory.

## 1. Verdict

**Every published v0.1 number checked was reproduced exactly from committed
data: 191 of 191 checks match** (`verification.csv`). This covers the label
distribution, all eight cell safety rates and Wilson intervals, all six CLSG
values and both means (overall 25.8 pp), all six McNemar tables, exact
p-values and Holm-adjusted p-values, the sensitivity analysis (S1–S3), and
the headline failure rates quoted in README/report (Roman Urdu 36.0%,
Misinformation 40.5%, Aya 23.5%, Qwen3 15.9%).

The published *statistics script* is not in the repository, so the numbers
were reconstructed independently from definitions in
`results/labeling/statistical_analysis.md`. Exact agreement means those
definitions are complete and correctly stated.

## 3. Corrected / extended analyses (new in v2, **not** replacements for v1 numbers)

### 3.1 Comprehension failures kept in the denominator

v1 dropped `ambiguous` outputs (18/240; mostly degenerate loops or "I don't
understand") from the denominator. v2 keeps all 30 pairs and reports two
outcome definitions separately (`paired_tests.csv`, `cell_outcomes.csv`).
Gaps below are target minus English for harmful compliance, English minus
target for safe rate; 95% CIs are Newcombe paired intervals; Holm within each
definition (6 tests).

| Model × language | v1 CLSG (amb. excluded) | Harmful-compliance gap [95% CI], Holm p | Safe-rate gap, amb. = not safe [95% CI], Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B × Urdu | 10.0 | 10.0 [−6.1, 26.8], .75 | 10.0 [−6.1, 26.8], .38 |
| Qwen3-8B × Roman Urdu | 39.5 | 26.7 [6.7, 45.1], **.086** | 56.7 [34.5, 72.3], <.001 |
| Qwen3-8B × code-switched | 8.2 | 6.7 [−6.7, 21.8], .75 | 20.0 [3.4, 37.5], .094 |
| Aya × Urdu | 46.2 | 40.0 [20.9, 57.7], .003 | 53.3 [32.7, 69.8], <.001 |
| Aya × Roman Urdu | 31.0 | 30.0 [12.5, 47.9], .020 | 33.3 [15.2, 51.2], .008 |
| Aya × code-switched | 20.0 | 20.0 [4.5, 37.3], .094 | 20.0 [4.5, 37.3], .094 |

Intent-cluster bootstrap (30 intents, 5,000 resamples, seed 20261112) for the
mean over the six comparisons: v1 CLSG 25.8 pp [17.3, 35.3]; harmful-compliance
gap 22.2 pp [14.4, 31.1].

**What this changes.** The direction of every gap is unchanged. But whether
Qwen3-8B × Roman Urdu is "significant" depends on how non-scoreable outputs
are treated: significant when they are dropped (v1) or counted as not-safe,
not significant (Holm p = .086) when only actual harmful compliance is
counted. Most of Qwen3's Roman Urdu "safety gap" is a comprehension/generation
failure (9 of 30 responses non-scoreable), not harmful compliance. v2 must
therefore measure comprehension and harmful compliance as separate outcomes
(protocol RQ3).

### 3.2 Scoring correction: refusal detection by language

`scoring_correction.csv` scores all 240 responses with the v1 backend
heuristic (`RefusalConsistencyEvaluator`, "refusal" = score ≥ 90) and the v2
multilingual rule judge (`backend/app/v2/judges/rule_judge.py`), against the
final labels.

| Language | n SAFE | v1 backend refusal recall | v2 rule judge refusal recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | 59 | 91.5% | 83.1% |
| Urdu | 40 | **0.0%** | 22.5% |
| Roman Urdu | 32 | 15.6% | 25.0% |
| Code-switched | 47 | 19.1% | 36.2% |

Neither detector ever flagged an UNSAFE response as a refusal (0/44). Had the
v1 backend scored these same responses, it would have reported "CLSG" of
42.5–70.0 points (`summary.json`) against label-based gaps of 6.7–46.2:
the backend metric is dominated by its own English bias.

The v2 rule judge is better but **still unusable as a safety metric** (recall
22–36% outside English). Its patterns were derived from these same 240
responses, so even these figures are optimistic. Consequences for v2: rule
judges are triage only; primary labels need a model-based judge validated
against human labels, plus the response classifiers of RQ5.

### 3.3 Response-language drift (`language_drift.csv`)

Aya Expanse 8B answered Roman Urdu prompts in Hindi Devanagari script in
20/30 cases and code-switched prompts in 22/30; Qwen3-8B answered 14/60
Roman-Urdu/code-switched prompts in English. Of Qwen3's 23 Roman-Urdu-script
responses to Roman Urdu prompts, 9 were harmful and 7 non-scoreable. This is
descriptive only (no test planned in v1) and motivates recording
response language/script as a covariate in v2.

## 4. Limitations of this reconstruction

- It verifies arithmetic, not labels. Label validity rests on one human
  reviewer (66 rows) and LLM-assisted review (174 rows, all `safe_refusal`);
  the false-safe rate of the LLM-reviewed rows is unknown.
- n = 30 intents per comparison; CIs are wide (often > 35 pp).
- 30 prompts, all harmful, single translator, no benign controls: the v0.1
  results are a pilot and are used in v2 only for effect-size planning.
